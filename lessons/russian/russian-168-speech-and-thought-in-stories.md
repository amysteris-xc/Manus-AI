## What you will be able to do
You will be able to insert short direct quotes and thoughts into simple fictional sentences using Russian quotation marks («…») and a few reporting verbs (сказать, подумать, ответить). You will also practice the small structural change when quoting questions or exclamations.

## Retrieval practice
1. Recall a Russian verb of motion in imperfective past: write the past-tense form of идти for "he went".
2. Recall the accusative singular for a feminine animate noun: give the form of сестра when used as a direct object.
3. Recall the instrumental singular form of a masculine noun: give the form of другом for "with a friend".
4. Recall a byte-boundary validation concept: what is a common reason to check UTF-8 byte boundaries when processing Cyrillic text?

## Core pattern
Direct speech in Russian typically uses guillemets «…» (or sometimes „…“), with a reporting clause before or after. Use simple reporting verbs: сказать (to say), спросить (to ask), ответить (to answer), подумать (to think — often used for internal thoughts). Punctuation inside the quotes follows normal sentence rules (question mark, exclamation, comma). If the reporting clause follows, the quoted sentence starts with a capital letter; if the reporting clause interrupts, punctuation and capitalization depend on structure.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Он сказал: «Я приду вечером». | He said, "I will come in the evening." |
| Она подумала: «Надо позвонить маме». | She thought, "I must call Mom." |
| Маша спросила: «Ты идёшь со мной?» | Masha asked, "Are you coming with me?" |

## Concise explanation
Form: Use quotation marks «…» to enclose the exact words or thought. Put a colon after the reporting verb when the quote follows (Он сказал: «…»). If the reporting clause comes after, end the quote with appropriate terminal punctuation (., ?, !) and then continue with a comma and the reporting clause if the sentence is arranged that way (less common in Russian than in English). Meaning: this embeds a speaker's exact words or a character's inner thought. Limitation/common confusion: do not convert direct quotes into indirect speech automatically — indirect speech in Russian requires changes in tense, mood, and often word order (e.g., Он сказал, что придёт), which is a separate topic; here we stick to direct quotation marks and simple reporting verbs.

## Worked dialogue
А: «Я опоздаю» — подумал Иван.
*He thought, "I'll be late."*

Б: «Почему ты опаздываешь?» — спросила Оля.
*"Why are you late?" asked Olya.*

А: «Пробки на дороге», — ответил он.
*"Traffic on the road," he answered.*

Б: «Постарайся быть пораньше», — сказала она.
*"Try to be earlier," she said.*

## Guided practice
1. Rewrite the sentence using a direct quote: Он заметил, что завтра занят. → transform to a direct quote with Он сказал: «…».
2. Insert a thought for a character named Лена using подумать and «…»: she thinks "I forgot the keys".
3. Turn the question "Ты видел ключи?" into a quoted question with the reporting verb спросил (for a male speaker).
4. Combine a short exclamation into a reporting clause: Мария воскликнула "Как красиво!" — correct punctuation and reporting placement.
5. Create a two-sentence micro-dialogue where one character says a line and another responds with a thought (use подумать for the thought).

## Independent production exercise
Write a 6–10 sentence fictional paragraph (short scene) in Russian that embeds at least three direct quotes or thoughts. Use at least two different reporting verbs from {сказать, спросить, ответить, подумать, воскликнуть}. Keep sentences simple (basic syntax, present or past tense). Provide an English translation under each Russian sentence (as in earlier sections).

Guidelines:
- Use quotation marks «…».
- Keep each quoted fragment short (one clause or sentence).
- Ensure punctuation inside quotes is correct (question marks for questions, exclamations for exclamations).
- Do not convert quotes into indirect speech.

You are ready to move on if you can reliably: place Russian quotations inside «…» with correct punctuation; use a reporting verb to introduce or follow a quote; distinguish direct quotes from indirect speech (and avoid converting one into the other inadvertently); and produce a short fictional paragraph meeting the Independent production task requirements. ## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. Model answer for Guided practice 1: Он сказал: «Я занят завтра». (Or: Он сказал: «Завтра я занят».) Both are acceptable; keep natural word order.
2. Model answer for Guided practice 2: Лена подумала: «Я забыла ключи».
3. Model answer for Guided practice 3: Он спросил: «Ты видел ключи?»
4. Model answer for Guided practice 4: Мария воскликнула: «Как красиво!» — correct punctuation is an exclamation inside the quotes and a colon after the reporting verb if the quote follows.
