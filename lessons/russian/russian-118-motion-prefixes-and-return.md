## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to recognize and use common Russian motion prefixes (при-, у-, вы-, по-, до-, and при- in return contexts) to describe arrival, departure, and simple return actions in short travel phrases and a brief travel narrative.

## Core pattern
Prefixed motion verbs are presented here as vocabulary items: base motion meaning + prefix = new lexical meaning related to arrival, departure, or movement completion.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| прийти | to arrive on foot / to come (perfective) |
| уехать | to depart by vehicle / to leave (perfective) |
| выехать | to drive out / to exit by vehicle (perfective) |
| поехать | to start going by vehicle (perfective, often "set off") |
| доехать | to reach by vehicle (perfective, arrive at destination) |

## Concise explanation
Form: take a motion verb stem (идти/ехать) and add a prefix (при-, у-, вы-, по-, до-) to create a prefixed perfective verb with a specific directional nuance: при- = arrive toward, у- = leave from, вы- = exit outward, по- = start a movement, до- = reach an endpoint. Meaning: treat each prefixed verb as a separate vocabulary item (you will often use them in perfective form to describe completed actions). Limitation/common confusion: prefixes can change meaning unpredictably for non-motion verbs and some motion prefixes overlap in meaning (e.g., прийти vs. подойти); when in doubt, learn the prefixed verb as a whole and pay attention to context and the transport (foot vs. vehicle).

## Worked dialogue
А: Я приехал в город вчера вечером поездом.
*I arrived in the city yesterday evening by train.*

Б: Ты откуда уехал утром?
*Where did you leave from in the morning?*

А: Я уехал из деревни рано, потому что хотел успеть на поезд.
*I left the village early because I wanted to catch the train.*

Б: А во сколько ты вернулся?
*And what time did you return?*

А: Я вернулся в город утром и сразу поехал на работу на автобусе.
*I returned to the city in the morning and immediately went to work by bus.*

## Guided practice — five numbered concrete tasks
1. Conjugate прийти in the past masculine, past feminine, and infinitive; write each form with an English gloss.
2. Write three short sentences (one for each) using: уехать, поехать, доехать. Each sentence must specify the transport (e.g., на автобусе) and include an English translation.
3. Change this sentence to indicate the start of movement rather than arrival: "Он приехал в Москву." Provide the Russian and English.
4. Read the worked dialogue and underline (in your copy) every prefixed verb; list them in Russian and give a one-word English gloss for each.
5. Create a two-sentence sequence describing: you left home, travelled by car, and reached a friend's house. Use at least one prefixed verb and provide English translations.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional travel paragraph (6–10 sentences) that tells a simple time-ordered story of a day trip. Include:
- a clear departure (use уехать or выехать),
- at least one transfer or mid-trip starting action (use поехать),
- an arrival (use приехать or доехать),
- and a return action (use вернуться with an appropriate prefixed motion verb if possible).
Make sure each Russian sentence has an immediate precise English translation on the next line. Use realistic, harmless details (times, transport) and keep sentences mostly simple.

You are ready to move on when you can: 1) recognize at least five prefixed motion verbs in a short text and give their basic meanings, 2) produce simple past-tense sentences describing leaving, starting to go, arriving, and returning with correct choice of prefix and transport phrase, and 3) explain in one sentence that prefixed motion verbs should often be learned as separate vocabulary items because prefixes change nuance. ## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. Conjugations of прийти: пришёл (masculine past) — "he arrived"; пришла (feminine past) — "she arrived"; прийти — "to arrive/to come" (infinitive).
2. Example sentences:
   - Я уехал на машине в шесть утра. — *I left by car at six in the morning.*
   - Мы поехали на автобусе после завтрака. — *We set off by bus after breakfast.*
   - Они доехали до станции за двадцать минут. — *They reached the station in twenty minutes.*
3. Change "Он приехал в Москву." to indicate start: "Он поехал в Москву." — *He set off for Moscow.* (Note: контекст and vehicle choice often imply поехал is by vehicle.)
4. Prefixed verbs from the dialogue list and gloss:
   - приехал — arrived
   - уехал — departed
   - вернулся — returned
   - поехал — set off / started going
5. Model two-sentence sequence:
   - Я выехал из дома на машине в семь утра. — *I drove out from home by car at seven in the morning.*
   - Я доехал до дома друга к девяти. — *I reached my friend's house by nine.*
