## Core pattern
Neutral word order in Russian is typically Subject — Verb — Object (SVO). Use SVO for straightforward statements. Changing order (e.g., putting the object first) is grammatical and shifts focus to the moved element; it does not usually change the basic meaning, only what is being highlighted.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Я читаю книгу. | I am reading a book. |
| Книга лежит на столе. | The book is lying on the table. |
| Он увидел Машу. | He saw Masha. |

## Concise explanation
Form: The neutral or unmarked order in modern colloquial Russian is Subject (S) — Verb (V) — Object (O), as in "Я читаю книгу." Meaning: this order presents information without special emphasis; the listener assumes no contrast or surprise on any element. Focus shift: placing an element at the beginning or end can mark it as contrastive or topical (e.g., "Книгу я читаю" highlights "the book" as the important or contrasted item). Limitation/common confusion: not every word order change is interchangeable in all contexts—changing order can sound formal, poetic, or marked, and some verbs or pronouns combine with clitic-like reductions that make unusual orders feel unnatural.

## Worked dialogue
А: Я читаю книгу.
*I am reading a book.*

Б: Книгу? А какую книгу?
*The book? Which book?*

А: Книгу про историю.
*A book about history.*

Б: А историю ты любишь?
*And do you like history?*

А: Да, историю я люблю.
*Yes, I do like history.*

## Guided practice
1. Convert these neutral SVO sentences into a version that emphasizes the object (put the object first) and translate both: "Она пишет письмо."
2. Take "Мы смотрим фильм вечером." Make a version that emphasizes "вечером" and explain the nuance in one sentence.
3. Change "Петя открыл дверь." to emphasize Петя (put subject first or use contrast) and explain why that sounds different.
4. Read this pair aloud and note the stress of focus: "Он купил хлеб." vs "Хлеб он купил." Write one sentence describing the difference in emphasis.
5. Create three original SVO sentences about everyday actions (6–10 words total each) and then reorder one element in each sentence to put focus on a different word; provide translations.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional description (6–10 sentences) about a morning routine in a small apartment. Use mostly neutral (SVO) sentences, but intentionally change the order in two sentences to highlight one detail each (for example, highlight the coffee or a neighbor). For each sentence, provide an English translation immediately after. Check that the changed-order sentences still make grammatical sense and note, in one short parenthetical, what is being emphasized.

## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. Example for Guided practice 1: Neutral: "Она пишет письмо." — "She is writing a letter." Object-first: "Письмо она пишет." — "It is the letter that she is writing." (focus on "letter")
2. Example for Guided practice 2: Neutral: "Мы смотрим фильм вечером." — "We watch a film in the evening." Emphasized time: "Вечером мы смотрим фильм." — "It is in the evening that we watch a film." (focus on time)
3. Example for Guided practice 3: Neutral: "Петя открыл дверь." — "Petya opened the door." Emphasized subject: "Петя открыл дверь." (same order but with intonation) or "Дверь открыл Петя." — "It was Petya who opened the door." (focus on actor)
4. Model independent production criteria: six to ten grammatically correct sentences; most SVO; exactly two sentences with changed word order that clearly emphasize an element; each Russian sentence followed by an accurate English translation and a short note naming the emphasized item.
