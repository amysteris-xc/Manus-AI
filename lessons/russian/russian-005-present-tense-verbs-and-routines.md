## 1. An infinitive names the action

A Russian dictionary usually lists a verb in its **infinitive** form, the form often translated with English “to …”. Three useful infinitives are:

| Infinitive | Meaning | Study pronunciation aid |
|---|---|---|
| рабо́тать | to work | *ra-bó-tat’* |
| чита́ть | to read | *chi-tát’* |
| учи́ться | to study | *u-chít’-sya* |

The ending **-ть** often appears in an infinitive. Do not expect the infinitive itself to fit after a subject in a present-tense statement. Russian changes the verb ending to show who performs the action.[1]

> **Meaning note:** Russian has one present-tense form for meanings such as “I work,” “I am working,” and “I do work.” Context supplies the difference.[1]

For this lesson, treat **учиться** as the useful phrase “to study.” It is a different verb from **учить** “to teach/learn something,” which is outside today’s scope.

## 2. Subject pronouns and present-tense endings

The subject tells who performs an action. Russian uses these core pronouns:

| Person | Pronoun | Plain English guide |
|---|---|---|
| First singular | я | I |
| Second singular, informal | ты | you |
| Third singular | он / она / оно | he / she / it |
| First plural | мы | we |
| Second plural or polite singular | вы | you |
| Third plural | они | they |

The verb ending usually matches the subject. Because the ending carries information, Russian can often omit the pronoun in a simple statement, especially **я**. Learn the full pronoun-plus-verb form first; use omission only after you can recognize the ending.

## 3. First pattern: работать and читать

**Работать** and **читать** use a useful beginner pattern. The endings show the person performing the action.[1]

| Subject | работать — to work | читать — to read |
|---|---|---|
| я | я рабо́таю | я чита́ю |
| ты | ты рабо́таешь | ты чита́ешь |
| он / она / оно | он/она/оно рабо́тает | он/она/оно чита́ет |
| мы | мы рабо́таем | мы чита́ем |
| вы | вы рабо́таете | вы чита́ете |
| они | они рабо́тают | они чита́ют |

The practical endings to notice are:

```text
я:      -ю / -аю
ты:     -ешь
он/она: -ет
мы:     -ем
вы:     -ете
они:    -ют
```

Do not rely on a single visual ending alone. Learn each verb in a small set with examples. A future lesson can add more conjugation patterns and exceptions.

## 4. A common study verb: учиться

**Учиться** has the meaning “to study.” It belongs to a different present-tense pattern, and its dictionary form includes **-ся**. Learn the entire set rather than trying to generate it from memory on the first day.[2]

| Subject | учиться — to study | Precise translation |
|---|---|---|
| я | я учу́сь | I study / I am studying |
| ты | ты у́чишься | you study — informal singular |
| он / она / оно | он у́чится; она у́чится | he/she studies |
| мы | мы у́чимся | we study |
| вы | вы у́читесь | you study — polite singular or plural |
| они | они у́чатся | they study |

Notice that the form changes more visibly than **работать**. The first-person form is **учу́сь**, not `*учиться`. The asterisk marks an incorrect learner form; do not use it as a Russian sentence.

For now, preserve the final **-сь/-ся** as part of the verb form:

```text
я учу́сь
ты у́чишься
она у́чится
```

Later lessons can explain reflexive verbs more fully. Today’s goal is accurate recognition and production of a small, high-value routine set.

## 5. Add three routine time words

These words let you place an action in a simple part of the day.

| Russian | Study pronunciation aid | Precise meaning |
|---|---|---|
| у́тром | *ú-tram* | in the morning |
| днём | *dnyom* | during the day / in the daytime |
| ве́чером | *vyé-che-ram* | in the evening |

A beginner-friendly word order is **time + subject + verb**:

```text
У́тром я рабо́таю.
In the morning, I work.

Днём я чита́ю.
During the day, I read.

Ве́чером я учу́сь.
In the evening, I study.
```

The subject pronoun may be omitted after you recognize the ending:

```text
Утром работаю.
Вечером учусь.
```

Both examples mean “I work in the morning” and “I study in the evening.” Keep **я** during early practice if it helps you build the pattern clearly.

## 6. Ask and answer about a routine

Use **Что ты де́лаешь?** to ask “What do you do?” informally. For a polite or plural question, use **Что вы де́лаете?**. Treat these as complete question patterns for now.

| Question | Answer model | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| Что ты де́лаешь у́тром? | У́тром я рабо́таю. | What do you do in the morning? — I work in the morning. |
| Что вы де́лаете ве́чером? | Ве́чером я учу́сь. | What do you do in the evening? — I study in the evening. |
| Ты чита́ешь днём? | Да, я чита́ю. | Do you read during the day? — Yes, I do. |

The phrase **Да, я читаю** is literally “Yes, I read,” but it is a natural short answer to the question. No separate word is required for English “do” here.

## 7. Worked dialogue: a three-part routine

Read the dialogue once slowly with stress marks. Then cover the English and say both speakers’ lines aloud.

> **А:** Здра́вствуйте! Что вы де́лаете у́тром?
> *Hello! What do you do in the morning?*
>
> **Б:** У́тром я рабо́таю. А вы?
> *In the morning, I work. And you?*
>
> **А:** Днём я чита́ю.
> *During the day, I read.*
>
> **Б:** А ве́чером?
> *And in the evening?*
>
> **А:** Ве́чером я учу́сь.
> *In the evening, I study.*
>
> **Б:** О́чень хорошо́!
> *Very good!*

Read the same dialogue without study accents:

```text
Здравствуйте! Что вы делаете утром?
Утром я работаю. А вы?
Днём я читаю.
А вечером?
Вечером я учусь.
Очень хорошо!
```

The dialogue reuses **Здравствуйте** and **Очень хорошо** from earlier lessons. The new content is the three time words and the present-tense verb forms.

## 9. Independent production exercise

Write a three-line routine using **утром**, **днём**, and **вечером**. Use only safe, non-sensitive information. An invented routine is equally useful; do not include work locations, account details, or any private schedule.

Use this framework:

```text
Утром я работаю.
Днём я читаю.
Вечером я учусь.
```

Then change exactly one line to a question-and-answer pair. Choose either informal **ты** or polite **вы**, and keep it consistent.

```text
Что вы делаете вечером?
Вечером я учусь.
```

Finally, say the routine aloud without English. On a second pass, cover the subject pronouns and rely on the verb endings to identify who performs each action.

1. **книга** is feminine; the matching pronoun is **она**.
2. **новое**.
3. **Это маленькое окно. Оно маленькое.**
4. In **тетрадь**, **ь** softens the preceding consonant. It does not by itself determine the noun’s gender.
5. The logical C string ends at its first `\0`. An index equal to or beyond `strlen(label)` is not a visible character in that string, even if the array has unused storage.
6. **работать** is the infinitive.
7. **я читаю**.
8. **ты работаешь**.
9. **она учится**.
10. **Вечером мы читаем.** means “In the evening, we read.”
11. **Что вы делаете?** is polite or plural.
12. **Вечером я учусь.**
13. A C string ends at the null character **`\0`**.
14. The polite routine question is **Что вы делаете вечером?**
15. A model invented routine is:

> Утром я работаю.
> Днём я читаю.
> Вечером я учусь.
