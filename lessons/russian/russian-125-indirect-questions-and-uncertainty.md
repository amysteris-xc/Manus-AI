## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:
- Form polite indirect yes/no questions using ли and report uncertainty with не знаю and кажется.
- Combine ли with embedded clauses to report hearsay or doubt (e.g., «Я не знаю, придёт ли он»).
- Use кажется to hedge statements politely and avoid overcommitting to information.

## Core pattern
Use ли to form indirect yes/no questions inside a larger sentence; use не знаю to explicitly say "I don't know"; use кажется to mean "it seems" or "apparently" and soften a claim.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Я не знаю, придёт ли он завтра. | I don't know whether he will come tomorrow. |
| Кажется, она уже ушла. | It seems she already left. / Apparently she already left. |
| Ты уверен, что он сказал «да»? — Я не знаю, сказал ли он «да». | Are you sure he said "yes"? — I don't know whether he said "yes." |

## Concise explanation
Form: For embedded yes/no questions, use ли immediately after the verb or clause where English would use whether: [main clause], [subordinate clause + ли]. Не знаю is followed by a subordinate clause: «Я не знаю, + (clause).» Кажется is a sentence-level hedge and usually precedes the clause it modifies: «Кажется, + clause.» Meaning: these forms report uncertainty or soften assertions. Limitation/common confusion: ли only marks yes/no indirect questions — do not use ли with wh-words (где, кто, когда). Wh-questions embed without ли (e.g., «Я не знаю, где он»). Also, кажется expresses perception, not certain fact; do not treat кажется as a synonym for proof.

## Worked dialogue
А: Ты знаешь, придёт ли Марина сегодня?
*Do you know whether Marina will come today?*
Б: Я не знаю. Кажется, у неё важная встреча.
*I don't know. It seems she has an important meeting.*
А: Она говорила, что позвонит. Ты слышал её сообщение?
*She said she would call. Did you hear her message?*
Б: Нет, я не слышал. Я не знаю, звонила ли она уже.
*No, I didn't hear it. I don't know whether she already called.*

## Guided practice
1. Translate into Russian: "I don't know if the bus is late." Use ли.
2. Translate into Russian: "It seems he forgot the keys." Use кажется.
3. Rewrite: Combine "Я не знаю. Он дома?" into one sentence using ли.
4. Make a polite question to ask a colleague whether they received a file, using ли in an indirect form.
5. Correct the mistake: Someone wrote «Я не знаю, где ли он». Explain and give the corrected sentence.

## Independent production exercise
Write 6–10 sentences about planning a small fictional meeting where information is uncertain. Include:
- At least two sentences using ли in subordinate clauses.
- At least one sentence with не знаю + subordinate clause.
- At least one sentence with кажется to hedge an assertion.
Keep sentences simple and factual (no real personal data). Example topic: planning a study group, inviting a friend, and uncertain arrival times.

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on when you can: (1) form an indirect yes/no clause with ли and place it correctly after the clause, (2) say "I don't know" with не знаю followed by a subordinate clause, and (3) use кажется to hedge statements without treating it as proof. If you still mix up ли with wh-words or forget the subordinate-clause word order, spend more time on Guided practice.

## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. "I don't know if the bus is late." — Я не знаю, опоздает ли автобус. (Accept similar word order: «Я не знаю, автобус опоздает ли» is less natural; place ли right after the verb/subordinate clause.)
2. "It seems he forgot the keys." — Кажется, он забыл ключи.
3. Combine "Я не знаю. Он дома?" — Я не знаю, дома ли он. (Also acceptable: «Я не знаю, он дома или нет.»)
4. Polite indirect question about a file: «Вы не знаете, получили ли вы файл?» or to a colleague: «Ты знаешь, получил ли он файл?» (Must include ли for yes/no subordinate clause.)
