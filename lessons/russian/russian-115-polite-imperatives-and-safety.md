## Core pattern
Short, polite imperative forms are built from the verb stem plus an imperative ending (informal singular, formal or plural), often softened with пожалуйста or будьте добры.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Подайте, пожалуйста, соль. | Please pass the salt. |
| Будьте добры, сядьте здесь. | Please be so kind as to sit here. |
| Откройте окно, пожалуйста. | Please open the window. |
| Подожди немного, пожалуйста. | Wait a little, please. |

## Concise explanation
Form: For most verbs, the informal singular imperative is formed from the 2nd person singular present stem (sometimes with a vowel change) or a special imperative form (e.g., "подай", "подожди"). The formal/plural polite form uses the 2nd person plural imperative ending (e.g., "подайте", "подождите") or the phrase "будьте добры" + infinitive to make a very courteous request. Meaning: These forms ask someone to do something; adding пожалуйста or будьте добры increases politeness. Limitation / common confusion: In Russian, using the informal singular imperative (ты form) with a stranger can sound rude; prefer the plural/formal imperative or "будьте добры" with people you do not know. Also, not every verb forms the imperative regularly (some have irregular stems), so consult references for irregular verbs.

## Worked dialogue
А: Подайте мне, пожалуйста, меню.
*Please pass me the menu, please.*

Б: Конечно, вот меню.
*Of course, here is the menu.*

А: Будьте добры, принесите счёт.
*Please be so kind as to bring the check.*

Б: Сейчас принесу.
*I'll bring it now.*

А: Подождите минуту, пожалуйста.
*Please wait a minute.*

Б: Хорошо, подожду.
*Okay, I will wait.*

## Guided practice — five numbered concrete tasks
1. Convert these verbs into a polite plural/formal imperative by adding appropriate endings and add пожалуйста: "дать", "открыть", "ждать". Write full Russian sentences.
2. Rewrite the following blunt command into a polite request using "будьте добры": "Сядьте здесь."
3. Translate into Russian a polite request: "Please close the door."
4. Listen or read aloud and mark which of these is more polite: "Подожди" vs "Подождите" — explain in one sentence why.
5. Role-play briefly with a partner (or write both lines): you ask someone to pass a pen politely; they reply that they will.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional 6–10 sentence exchange between two coworkers in the office using polite imperatives. Include:
- At least two different verbs in imperative forms.
- Use пожалуйста at least twice and будьте добры once.
- Keep the situation ordinary and safe (e.g., passing documents, opening a window, waiting for a meeting).
Example requirements: produce natural, grammatical Russian sentences; after each Russian sentence, include a precise English translation (italicized or on the next line).
