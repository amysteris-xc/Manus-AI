## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to write and speak 6–10 sentence opening paragraphs that clearly establish when, where, and briefly why a short fictional scene takes place, using basic past-tense phrases and common location expressions.

2. Recall and write a Russian sentence using "в" + accusative to indicate movement into a place (from Russian 096–101), e.g., "She walked into the room."
3. Recall and write a Russian sentence with a past verb and a time phrase like "вчера" or "в прошлом году" (from Russian 096–101).
4. Recall a C concept: explain briefly why validating input length in bytes matters for UTF-8 strings when enforcing a 256-byte limit.

## Core pattern
Brief teaching: To set a scene, combine a past-tense clause about time, a locative or directional phrase for place, and a short background clause (reason or state). Use past verbs (e.g., был/была/были, пришёл/пришла/пришли, сидел/сидела/сидели) and location words (в, на, у, рядом с, в комнате, в парке). Keep sentences simple.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Был поздний вечер, и улицы были пустыми. | It was late evening, and the streets were empty. |
| Она пришла в маленькую комнату у старого дома. | She came into a small room by the old house. |
| Рядом с окном стоял стол с книгами. | A table with books stood near the window. |

## Concise explanation
Form: Use the past tense (он/она/они + past participle) and common prepositions for place (в, на, у, рядом с). Meaning: These patterns state when something happened, where it happened, and give a brief background object or situation. Limitation/common confusion: Russian past tense agrees in gender and number (я был/я была) — be careful to match the subject. Also, "в" vs "на" depends on set expressions and can change meaning; if unsure, use "в" for enclosed spaces (в комнате, в доме) and "на" for surfaces or named open places (на площади, на поле) but remember there are exceptions.

## Worked dialogue
А: Был холодный вечер, и свет в доме был тёплым.
*It was a cold evening, and the light in the house was warm.*

Б: Она сидела у окна и смотрела на пустую улицу.
*She sat by the window and looked at the empty street.*

А: На столе лежала старая карта, и рядом был закрытый чемодан.
*An old map lay on the table, and a closed suitcase was nearby.*

Б: В доме никого не было, только ветер шуршал в саду.
*There was nobody in the house, only the wind was rustling in the garden.*

## Guided practice
1. Write one sentence in Russian that says "It was morning" and includes a location (e.g., "on the balcony" or "in the kitchen").
2. Change the sentence to add a short background clause explaining why the character was there (one short clause starting with "потому что" or "чтобы").
3. Write two sentences describing a room using past-tense verbs and two location phrases (e.g., "на столе", "рядом с дверью").
4. Replace one past-tense sentence with a negation (e.g., "никто не был" or "не было").
5. Read your four sentences aloud and record the gender agreement: mark whether the subject is masculine/feminine/neuter/plural and check past-tense endings.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional opening (6–10 sentences) that establishes when and where a scene takes place and gives a brief background reason for the character's presence. Use:
- At least two past-tense verbs (agreeing in gender/number),
- At least two location phrases (в/на/у/рядом с),
- One time phrase (e.g., "в прошлом году", "одним вечером", "поздним утром"),
- One short clause of motivation or circumstance (e.g., "потому что", "чтобы").

Keep language simple and accurate; aim for clarity of setting rather than ornate style.

If you still make repeated agreement errors or misuse "в" vs "на", repeat the guided practice and spaced items.
