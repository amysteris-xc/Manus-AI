## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to state what a fictional character wants and why, using simple verbs of desire and purpose phrases (e.g., хотеть + infinitive, чтобы + clause, ради + noun). You will also practice distinguishing the character’s intention from the actual outcome.

## Core pattern
We describe: (1) what a character wants, and (2) why or for what purpose, and (3) optionally contrast with what actually happens.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Он хочет купить велосипед, чтобы ездить на работу. | He wants to buy a bicycle in order to ride to work. |
| Она хочет сдать экзамен ради спокойствия. | She wants to pass the exam for the sake of peace of mind. |
| Они хотят, чтобы родители были довольны. | They want their parents to be pleased. |

## Concise explanation
Use хотеть + infinitive to state a desire: subject + хочет/хотят + infinitive. To express purpose, use чтобы + clause (subjunctive-like) or ради + noun. To state a desired outcome for another person, use хотеть, чтобы + clause with a verb in the correct person and tense. A common confusion: чтобы is not interchangeable with для того чтобы in casual sentences — both express purpose, but для того чтобы is more explicit and slightly more formal. Also note: "хотеть" does not guarantee outcome — you should separate desire from result with contrasts like но (but) or но в итоге (but in the end).

## Worked dialogue
А: Я хочу найти новую работу, чтобы меньше ездить в пробках.
*I want to find a new job so I drive less in traffic.*

Б: Но у тебя нет времени на поиск — ты очень занят.
*But you don't have time to search — you're very busy.*

А: Я знаю, но я стараюсь отправлять резюме по вечерам.
*I know, but I'm trying to send resumes in the evenings.*

Б: Надеюсь, это поможет; в итоге ты будешь спокойнее.
*I hope this will help; in the end you will be calmer.*

## Guided practice
1. Translate into Russian: "She wants to move to a smaller apartment to save money."
2. Write a sentence in Russian: "They want their teacher to see the project."
3. Rewrite this intention to show a different outcome: "Он хочет выиграть конкурс, но не прошёл в финал." (Change the event to a different ordinary outcome).
4. Create a two-sentence mini-description of a character (2nd or 3rd person) stating a goal and the reason using ради + noun.
5. Ask a question in Russian to find out someone’s motivation: begin with Почему or Зачем and include хотеть.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional paragraph (6–10 sentences) about a single character. For that character:
- State at least two desires using хотеть + infinitive or хотеть, чтобы + clause.
- Give at least two reasons or purposes (use чтобы, ради, or для того чтобы).
- Include one sentence that contrasts intention and outcome (e.g., но, в итоге, однако).
Keep the motivations ordinary (study, work, family, hobbies). Use correct agreement and simple past or present forms you know.

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on if you can: (1) produce at least three correct sentences stating desires and purposes, (2) use хочеть + infinitive and хотеть, чтобы + clause with correct agreement, and (3) explicitly contrast a desire with an outcome in one sentence. If you make repeated errors with agreement or clause formation, review the Core pattern and Concise explanation, then redo the Guided practice.
