## 1. A Russian yes/no question keeps the statement’s word order

English commonly adds “do” or moves a verb to form a yes/no question:

```text
You read at the library.
Do you read at the library?
```

Everyday Russian does not add a word matching English “do,” and it does not invert subject and verb for this kind of question. Say the normal statement pattern with question intonation; in writing, use a question mark.[1]

```text
Ты читаешь в библиотеке.
You read at the library.

Ты читаешь в библиотеке?
Do you read at the library?
```

The words and order are the same. When speaking, raise your voice noticeably on the word that carries the question’s focus. For a simple neutral question, that is often the verb or the final important word. Do not treat an English rising tone as an exact model; listen and imitate Russian carefully over time.

> **Question rule for today:** Reuse the statement you already know. Do not add a Russian word for “do,” and do not reverse the subject and verb.

## 2. Turn familiar statements into questions

Use the routine and location statements from Russian 005–006. The punctuation changes in writing; the intonation changes in speech.

| Statement | Yes/no question | Precise question meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ты дома. | Ты дома? | Are you at home? |
| Ты читаешь в библиотеке. | Ты читаешь в библиотеке? | Do you read at the library? |
| Ты учишься в школе. | Ты учишься в школе? | Do you study at school? |
| Вы работаете в школе. | Вы работаете в школе? | Do you work at a school? — polite singular or plural |

The pronoun **ты** is informal singular. **Вы** is polite singular or plural. Preserve that choice through the question and its answer.

```text
Ты дома?
Are you at home? — informal

Вы дома?
Are you at home? — polite singular or plural
```

## 3. Answer clearly with `да` and `нет`

**Да** means “yes,” and **нет** means “no.” For early practice, pair either word with a short restatement. This makes the answer clear and gives you more useful speaking practice than a bare one-word reply.[1]

| Question | Clear affirmative answer | Clear negative answer |
|---|---|---|
| Ты дома? | Да, я дома. | Нет, я не дома. |
| Ты читаешь в библиотеке? | Да, я читаю в библиотеке. | Нет, я не читаю в библиотеке. |
| Вы учитесь в школе? | Да, я учусь в школе. | Нет, я не учусь в школе. |

A bare **да** or **нет** can be enough in a simple situation. The full answer is safer while learning because it confirms both the meaning and the verb form.

## 4. Use `не` to make a basic negative statement

**Не** means “not.” In a simple action sentence, place it directly before the verb.[2]

```text
Я читаю в библиотеке.
I read at the library.

Я не читаю в библиотеке.
I do not read at the library.
```

The verb still agrees with the subject. Negation does not erase the ordinary present-tense ending.

| Positive statement | Negative statement | Precise translation |
|---|---|---|
| Я работаю. | Я не работаю. | I work. / I do not work. |
| Ты читаешь. | Ты не читаешь. | You read. / You do not read. |
| Я учусь дома. | Я не учусь дома. | I study at home. / I do not study at home. |

When there is no present-tense verb in the location statement, **не** comes before the part you are negating:

```text
Я дома.
I am at home.

Я не дома.
I am not at home.
```

For this lesson, memorize **я не дома** as a whole short response. A later lesson can expand negative patterns and their nuances.

## 5. Do not invent an English-style auxiliary

These forms are useful:

```text
Ты читаешь?
Do you read? — literally, “You read?” with question intonation

Ты не читаешь?
Do you not read? / Don’t you read? — use carefully and answer clearly
```

Do **not** add a separate Russian word for English “do,” and do not reverse the pronoun and verb.

```text
Ты читаешь?
Correct everyday question

*Читаешь ты?
Not the neutral beginner pattern for “Do you read?”
```

The asterisk marks a form that is not the target pattern for this lesson. Russian word order can change for emphasis in advanced contexts, but the dependable beginner model is **Ты читаешь?**

## 6. Worked dialogue: confirm a routine and location

Read the dialogue once with stress marks. Then cover the English and say both roles aloud. The dialogue uses only previously learned verbs, locations, and greetings plus the new **не** pattern.

> **А:** Здра́вствуйте! Вы чита́ете в библиоте́ке?
> *Hello! Do you read at the library?*
>
> **Б:** Да, я чита́ю в библиоте́ке.
> *Yes, I read at the library.*
>
> **А:** Вы учи́тесь в шко́ле?
> *Do you study at school?*
>
> **Б:** Нет, я не учу́сь в шко́ле. Ве́чером я учу́сь до́ма.
> *No, I do not study at school. In the evening, I study at home.*
>
> **А:** О́чень хорошо́!
> *Very good!*

Read the same dialogue without study accents:

```text
Здравствуйте! Вы читаете в библиотеке?
Да, я читаю в библиотеке.
Вы учитесь в школе?
Нет, я не учусь в школе. Вечером я учусь дома.
Очень хорошо!
```

Notice the contrast: **не учусь в школе** denies the school location, while **учусь дома** supplies a different location. The verb form remains **учусь** in both statements.

## 8. Independent production exercise

Write and say two short question-and-answer exchanges. Use real or invented general routines; do not include exact locations, schedules, addresses, school names, or workplace details.

The first exchange must be affirmative. The second must be negative and must use **не** before a familiar verb.

```text
Ты читаешь в библиотеке?
Да, я читаю в библиотеке.

Вы учитесь в школе?
Нет, я не учусь в школе.
```

Then create one location-only exchange:

```text
Ты дома?
Нет, я не дома.
```

Read each answer aloud twice. On the second pass, cover the English and check that every negative action uses **не** before the verb.
