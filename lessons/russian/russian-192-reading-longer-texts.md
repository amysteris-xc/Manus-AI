## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:
- Identify headings and key words to predict a text's content.
- Formulate a single main-idea question to guide reading.
- Skim for structure, then scan for answers while marking uncertain parts for later review.
- Use simple annotation marks to capture degrees of certainty without doing a full translation.

## Core pattern
A practical pattern for approaching a longer Russian text: identify headings, underline key words, write one main-idea question, then skim and scan. Use three simple annotation marks: ? for uncertainty, * for likely main points, and // for contrasts.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Заголовки помогают предсказать содержание. | Headings help predict the content. |
| Подчёркивайте ключевые слова и фразы. | Underline key words and phrases. |
| Сформулируйте один главный вопрос перед чтением. | Formulate one main question before reading. |

## Concise explanation
Form: short imperative statements or tips that tell you what to do with the text (identify, underline, ask). Meaning: these steps convert passive reading into goal-directed activity so you read to answer a specific question rather than to translate every word. Limitation/common confusion: this strategy helps comprehension and efficiency, but it does not replace careful translation when you need precise wording (for legal or technical accuracy).

## Worked dialogue
А: Как лучше начать чтение длинной статьи?
*How is it best to start reading a long article?*

Б: Сначала прочтите заголовки и подзаголовки, чтобы понять структуру.
*First read the headings and subheadings to understand the structure.*

А: Что делать с незнакомыми словами?
*What should I do with unfamiliar words?*

Б: Подчеркните их и пометьте знаком вопроса, но продолжайте сканировать текст для ответа на главный вопрос.
*Underline them and mark with a question sign, but continue scanning the text for the main question's answer.*

А: Как узнать главный вопрос?
*How do I find the main question?*

Б: Сформулируйте одно предложение: «О чём эта статья?» и читайте, чтобы ответить на него.
*Formulate one sentence: "What is this article about?" and read to answer it.*

## Guided practice
1. Take a 400–600-word Russian article (news, blog, or short essay). Write down its main heading and two subheadings.
2. Underline five key words or phrases you think are central to the article and translate only those five words.
3. Write one concise main-idea question in Russian (one sentence) about the article.
4. Skim the article in 3 minutes, marking passages you think answer the main question with * and uncertain parts with ?.
5. Scan the article for 5 sentences that support your answer; copy them and give a one-line summary in English for each.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short 6–10 sentence paragraph in Russian about how you would approach reading a technical paragraph (e.g., a protocol description). Use headings, list two key words you would look for, and include one main-idea question. Mark any words you are unsure about with a question mark. Provide an English translation sentence-by-sentence after the Russian paragraph.

## Spaced-repetition plan
| When | Task |
|---|---|
| About one hour later | Re-skim the same article and compare your initial marks (*) and (?) with what you now understand. |
| Next day | Revisit your main-idea question and write a one-sentence answer in Russian. |
| Three days later | Find a new 400–600-word text and apply the same strategy; time your skim to 3 minutes. |
| One week later | Review two previous articles' annotations and refine translations for the five key words you originally underlined. |

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on if you can: identify headings and three key words in a 400–600-word Russian text, write a clear main-idea question in Russian, and mark where the text answers that question while distinguishing uncertain segments for later precise lookup. If you find yourself translating every sentence, practice skimming faster and focusing on structure instead of full translation.
