## Core pattern

Russian plural past-tense forms do not divide into masculine and feminine forms. The familiar verbs use **-ли**: работали, читали, учились. Keep the sentence content general and avoid reporting real people’s private routines.

| Infinitive | Plural past form | Example | Precise translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| работать | работали | Мы работали. | We worked. |
| читать | читали | Студенты читали книги. | The students read books. |
| учиться | учились | Они учились дома. | They studied at home. |

Read each Russian example aloud twice. Then cover the English and produce the meaning from the Russian. Keep the whole phrase together rather than translating one word at a time.

## Concise explanation

Start with the model sentence in the table. Identify the word that carries the new grammar pattern, then keep familiar words such as **я**, **не**, **читать**, **учиться**, **дома**, and **вечером** in their established forms. This reduces the chance of changing several grammatical features at once.

Russian word order can change for context and emphasis, but the neutral beginner order remains dependable: time or subject, then the verb or grammar phrase, then the remaining information. Use a question mark and Russian question intonation for a yes/no question. Do not invent an English-style auxiliary.

## Worked dialogue

> **А:** Студенты читали книги?
> *Did the students read books?*
>
> **Б:** Да, студенты читали книги.
> *Yes, the students read books.*
>
> **А:** Они учились в школе?
> *Did they study at school?*
>
> **Б:** Нет, они не учились в школе.
> *No, they did not study at school.*

Cover the English translation. Read both roles aloud, then replace one ordinary noun or activity while retaining the new pattern exactly.

## Independent production exercise

Create three fictional group statements with **мы** or **они** and one of **работали**, **читали**, or **учились**. Use general locations such as **дома** or no location at all.

After writing, cover the English meanings and read the Russian twice. On the second reading, underline the new pattern in every sentence. If a sentence has more than one unfamiliar change, shorten it and rebuild it from a familiar model.
