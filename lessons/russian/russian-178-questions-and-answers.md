## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:
- Use short turn-taking markers to signal you want to speak or to invite continuation (e.g., «Подождите», «Да?»).
- Ask follow-up questions that clarify or probe (e.g., «Почему вы так думаете?»; «Что именно вы имеете в виду?»).
- Answer a follow-up with a clear claim, a reason, and a limit (e.g., «Да, это верно, потому что… но …»).
- Restate a partner’s point briefly to check understanding (e.g., «Вы говорите, что…?»).
- Avoid a common confusion point: don't overuse question particles — Russian often prefers intonation and short phrases.

## Core pattern
Teaching paragraph: When you ask a follow-up question in Russian, combine a short turn-taking marker, a restatement of the interlocutor’s point, and then the actual probe. When answering, follow the pattern: claim (Короткое утверждение) + reason (Пояснение) + limit (Ограничение). Use intonation and small particles to manage turns.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| «Подождите: вы говорите, что проект завершён?» | "Wait: are you saying the project is finished?" |
| «Да, это верно, потому что мы закончили все тесты, но осталось документирование.» | "Yes, that is true because we finished all the tests, but documentation remains." |
| «Что именно осталось документировать?» | "What exactly remains to be documented?" |

## Concise explanation
Form: A follow-up question often begins with a turn-taking marker (e.g., «Подождите», «Стоп», «Минуточку», «Да?»), then a brief restatement using the verb in the same tense as the original utterance, then the question word or probe. Meaning: This sequence signals you heard the interlocutor and now request clarification. Limitation/common confusion: English uses auxiliary verbs and rising intonation more frequently; in Russian, particles and word order change are limited — do not try to translate English inversion word-for-word. Also, when giving a claim-reason-limit answer, keep the limit short and factual; complex subordinate clauses can make speech heavy for listeners.

## Worked dialogue
А: «Подождите, вы предлагаете перенести встречу на завтра?»
*Wait, are you proposing to move the meeting to tomorrow?*

Б: «Да, это предложение верно, потому что все ресурсы свободны завтра, но не позже 16:00.»
*Yes, that proposal is correct because all resources are available tomorrow, but not after 4:00 PM.*

А: «То есть вы свободны до 16:00 — это единственное ограничение?»
*So you are free until 4:00 PM — that is the only limitation?*

Б: «Да, ровно: свободны до 16:00; если будет необходимость, можно сократить повестку.»
*Yes, exactly: free until 4:00 PM; if necessary, we can shorten the agenda.*

## Guided practice
1. Replace «Подождите» with three other Russian turn-taking markers in a sentence asking for clarification. Write the sentences in Russian and translate them.
2. Restate this sentence in a confirmation question: «Мы начнём презентацию в 10:00.» Make the restatement and a short probe asking for the agenda.
3. Write an answer that follows claim + reason + limit for the question «Можно ли завершить отчёт сегодня?» (in Russian).
4. Convert the English follow-up question "Do you mean the budget or the schedule?" into natural Russian with restatement.
5. Practice aloud: record (or say) a 6-turn mini-dialogue with a partner using turn-taking markers, restatement, one follow-up question, and one claim/reason/limit answer.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional dialog (6–10 sentences total, any mix of speakers) set in an academic office where one person proposes a small change to a research plan and the other asks follow-up questions. Include:
- One explicit turn-taking marker.
- One restatement of the proposal in question form.
- One follow-up question that probes the reason.
- One answer that contains a clear claim, a reason, and a limit.
Write the entire dialog in Russian; provide an English translation line for each Russian sentence.
