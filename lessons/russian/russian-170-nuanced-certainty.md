## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will reliably choose between words and phrases that express strong certainty (точно), probable belief (наверное), tentative impression (кажется), and explicit lack of certainty (не уверен(а)). You will match the strength of your Russian wording to the strength of the evidence you have.

## Core pattern
Concise teaching: Use точнo when you have direct evidence or certainty. Use наверное when you infer something reasonably likely. Use кажется when you have a subjective impression or weak evidence. Use не уверен(а) to state explicit doubt or lack of knowledge. Match your phrase to how confident you actually are.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Я точно знаю, что поезд в шесть. | I know for sure that the train is at six. |
| Наверное, он опоздает на десять минут. | He will probably be ten minutes late. |
| Кажется, у них сейчас мало клиентов. | It seems they have few customers right now. |
| Я не уверен(а), что это правильный адрес. | I am not sure that this is the correct address. |

## Concise explanation
Form: each expression is a short adverb or phrase that modifies the speaker’s epistemic stance. Meaning: точнo expresses factual certainty or high-confidence knowledge; наверное signals inference with a reasonable probability (often ~70–90% depending on context); кажется indicates a personal impression or low-confidence observation; не уверен(а) explicitly marks uncertainty. Limitation/common confusion: Russian speakers sometimes use наверное colloquially where English would use "probably" with lower confidence — context and intonation matter. Also, кажется often implies subjectivity and can be followed by что + clause or used alone with a clause; do not treat these words as exact numeric probabilities.

## Worked dialogue
А: Я точно видел её в кафе в три часа.
*I definitely saw her at the café at three o'clock.*

Б: Наверное, это была она, но я не уверен.
*Probably it was her, but I'm not sure.*

А: Кажется, кафе уже закрыто в воскресенье.
*It seems the café is already closed on Sunday.*

Б: Я не уверен(а); давай проверим на сайте.
*I am not sure; let's check on the website.*

## Guided practice
1. Replace the English bracket with an appropriate Russian phrase: "He [probably] forgot the meeting." — write a Russian sentence using наверное.
2. Write a sentence in Russian using точно to assert a time or fact you would be certain about (e.g., scheduled appointment).
3. Create two Russian sentences about the weather: one beginning with кажется and one with не уверен(а), showing the difference in evidence strength.
4. Read the short scenario: "You see someone's umbrella but not the person." Write one Russian sentence saying what you infer (use наверное) and one saying you are not sure (use не уверен(а)).
5. Convert this English into natural Russian: "I am sure the address is correct." Then make it weaker: "I think the address is probably correct."

## Independent production exercise
Write a short fictional paragraph (6–10 sentences) in Russian about planning to meet a friend in the city. Use all four target expressions at least once across the paragraph: точно, наверное, кажется, and не уверен(а). Make sure each Russian sentence has a clear signal (time, observation, or lack of information) that justifies the chosen expression. Provide an English translation sentence-by-sentence immediately after each Russian sentence.

Example constraints to follow:
- One sentence must state a fact you are certain about (use точно).
- One sentence must state an inference from indirect evidence (use наверное).
- One sentence must express an impression without hard evidence (use кажется).
- One sentence must explicitly say you are not sure about something (use не уверен(а)).
- Keep all sentences simple to intermediate in grammar (no complex subordinate chains beyond что + clause).
