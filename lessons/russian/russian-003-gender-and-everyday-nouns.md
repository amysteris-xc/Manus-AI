## 1. Gender is a grammar category

Every Russian noun has a grammatical gender: **masculine**, **feminine**, or **neuter**.[1] For people, gender often aligns with the person’s sex. For objects, it is a grammar property rather than a claim about the object itself. For example, **стол** “table” is masculine, while **книга** “book” is feminine.[2]

At this stage, gender helps you choose a pronoun. Later, it will also affect adjective endings, past-tense verbs, and other forms. Learn each new noun with its gender and its stressed pronunciation, not as an isolated English translation.

> **Learning habit:** Record nouns as a small package: `стол — masculine — table`, not merely `стол — table`.

## 2. A first gender map from the dictionary form

Use the noun’s **dictionary form**, also called the nominative singular, for these first patterns. The ending gives a strong clue but not a guarantee in every word.

| Ending in dictionary form | Usual gender | Examples | First pronunciation aid |
|---|---|---|---|
| A consonant or **й** | Masculine | стол — table; дом — house; чай — tea | *stol*; *dom*; *chai* |
| **а** or **я** | Feminine | кни́га — book; ру́чка — pen; неде́ля — week | *kní-ga*; *rúch-ka*; *ni-dyé-lya* |
| **о** or **е** | Neuter | окно́ — window; мо́ре — sea; письмо́ — letter | *ak-nó*; *mó-re*; *pi-s’mó* |
| **ь** | Masculine **or** feminine | день — day; ночь — night | *dyen’*; *noch’* |

These patterns apply to the dictionary form. Russian noun endings change in other grammatical situations, which later lessons will cover. For now, look only at the form listed in a dictionary or vocabulary list.

## 3. Treat the soft sign as an information gap

The final soft sign **ь** does not itself tell you whether a noun is masculine or feminine. It can mark either gender.[1] You must learn the word’s gender with the word.

| Noun | Meaning | Gender | Memory cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| день | day | masculine | `день — он` |
| словарь | dictionary | masculine | `словарь — он` |
| ночь | night | feminine | `ночь — она` |
| тетрадь | notebook | feminine | `тетрадь — она` |

Do not try to hear the gender from the sound of **ь**. Its role here is pronunciation-related: it softens the preceding consonant. Gender comes from the word’s grammatical pattern, so record it explicitly.

## 4. A few important exceptions

Endings are useful clues, but they are not a replacement for learning vocabulary carefully.

| Noun | Meaning | Ending clue | Actual gender | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| папа | dad | **а** often suggests feminine | masculine | A male person noun. |
| дядя | uncle | **я** often suggests feminine | masculine | A male person noun. |
| мужчина | man | **а** often suggests feminine | masculine | A male person noun. |
| кофе | coffee | **е** often suggests neuter | masculine | A common notable exception. |

For beginners, the practical solution is simple: learn the noun together with **он** (masculine), **она** (feminine), or **оно** (neuter). This avoids trying to reason from an ending when the word is an exception.

## 6. Refer back with он, она, and оно

Russian pronouns can match the gender of the noun they replace.

| Noun | Gender | Pronoun | Literal first use |
|---|---|---|---|
| стол | masculine | он | it / he |
| книга | feminine | она | it / she |
| окно | neuter | оно | it |

For objects, translate **он**, **она**, and **оно** as “it.” Do not infer that a table is male or a book is female. The Russian pronoun simply follows grammatical gender.

A small, repeatable sentence pattern is:

```text
Э́то [noun]. [Pronoun] зде́сь.
This is [noun]. It is here.
```

| Russian | Translation |
|---|---|
| Э́то стол. Он зде́сь. | This is a table. It is here. |
| Э́то кни́га. Она́ зде́сь. | This is a book. It is here. |
| Э́то окно́. Оно́ зде́сь. | This is a window. It is here. |

**Это** is a useful introductory word meaning “this is” or “this.” Treat the first three patterns as whole expressions. You do not need a full theory of Russian word order yet.

## 7. Worked dialogue: identify objects around you

Read the dialogue once with stress marks. Then cover the English and answer each question aloud.

> **А:** Что э́то?
> *What is this?*
>
> **Б:** Э́то кни́га.
> *This is a book.*
>
> **А:** Кни́га? Она́ зде́сь?
> *A book? Is it here?*
>
> **Б:** Да, она́ зде́сь.
> *Yes, it is here.*
>
> **А:** А что э́то?
> *And what is this?*
>
> **Б:** Э́то окно́. Оно́ зде́сь.
> *This is a window. It is here.*

Read the same exchange without stress marks:

```text
Что это?
Это книга.
Книга? Она здесь?
Да, она здесь.
А что это?
Это окно. Оно здесь.
```

The questions are intentionally simple. The goal is to pair a visible object with its Russian name and gender-linked pronoun.

## 9. Independent production exercise

Choose six safe, familiar objects around you, in a room, or in an imagined room. Look up each Russian noun in a reliable dictionary or course source. Do not use personal details. Make a three-column inventory:

| Russian noun | Gender package | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| кни́га | feminine — она | book |
| стол | masculine — он | table |
| окно́ | neuter — оно | window |

Then write and say three paired sentences using one noun of each gender:

```text
Это [masculine noun]. Он здесь.
Это [feminine noun]. Она здесь.
Это [neuter noun]. Оно здесь.
```

Finally, cover the gender column and test yourself. Say the noun, then supply **он**, **она**, or **оно** before uncovering your answer.

1. A polite greeting: **Здравствуйте!**
2. “My name is …”: **Меня зовут …**
3. “Where are you from?” politely: **Откуда вы?**
4. The soft sign **ь** has no independent sound; it usually softens the preceding consonant.
5. In C, `=` assigns and `==` compares.
6. A noun ending in a consonant or **й** is usually masculine.
7. A noun ending in **о** or **е** is usually neuter.
8. **ручка** is feminine, so use **она**.
9. **письмо** is neuter, so use **оно**.
10. **ночь** may be masculine or feminine in principle because **ь** occurs with both; this particular word is feminine and must be learned as **ночь — она**.
11. **папа** is masculine.
12. **Это стол. Он здесь.**
13. C 002 uses `fgets` to place input in a bounded, inspectable buffer before conversion. That permits checks for extra text and an overlong line.
