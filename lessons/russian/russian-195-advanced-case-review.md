## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to select and use the correct Russian cases to express possession, destination, accompaniment (companion), recipient, and topic in short, natural sentences.

2. Recall a past pattern from Russian 096–101: form a simple accusative for direction with в/на: "I go to the library" in Russian.
3. Recall a past pattern from Russian 096–101: use the instrumental for accompaniment: "She came with a friend" in Russian.

## Core pattern
This short scene keeps vocabulary limited and focuses on five functions: possession (у + genitive), destination (accusative with в/на), companion (instrumental), recipient (dative), and topic (о/об + prepositional). Use these reliable patterns in mixed sentences.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| У меня есть книга. | I have a book. |
| Я иду в кафе. | I am going to the café. |
| Он пришёл с другом. | He came with a friend. |
| Я дал письмо сестре. | I gave the letter to (my) sister. |
| Мы говорим о проекте. | We are talking about the project. |

## Concise explanation
Forms used here are common and regular: possession is expressed with у + genitive (у меня, у тебя, у него/неё), destination with directional accusative after в/на (в театр, на работу), accompaniment with instrumental (с другом, с подругой), recipient with dative (сестре, коллеге), and topic with о/об + prepositional (о проекте). Limitation: some verbs and prepositions require different cases or alternate prepositions (e.g., идти vs. ехать nuances, or verbs that take genitive objects), so use this lesson as a controlled review rather than covering every exception.

## Worked dialogue
А: У меня есть карта, ты идёшь в музей?
*I have a map, are you going to the museum?*

Б: Да, я иду в музей с другом.
*Yes, I'm going to the museum with a friend.*

А: Можно я отдам тебе билет? Он для тебя.
*Can I give you the ticket? It is for you.*

Б: Спасибо — дай билет мне, пожалуйста. Потом мы поговорим о выставке.
*Thank you — give the ticket to me, please. Later we'll talk about the exhibition.*

## Guided practice
1. Replace the noun in "У меня есть книга." with "телефон" and write the full sentence in Russian and English.
2. Change destination: transform "Я иду в кафе." into "I am going to the university" in Russian.
3. Form a sentence expressing accompaniment: "She came with her sister" in Russian and English.
4. Make a short line giving a recipient: "I will send the document to the manager" in Russian.
5. Create two sentences about topic: one using о and one using об (choose a word that requires об), with English translations.

## Independent production exercise
Write a 6–10 sentence fictional scene (in Russian), using Cyrillic, that includes:
- one sentence showing possession with у + genitive,
- one sentence indicating destination with в/на + accusative,
- one sentence showing accompaniment with instrumental,
- one sentence with a recipient in dative,
- one sentence discussing a topic with о/об,
- and one sentence that combines two functions (for example: destination + companion, or possession + recipient).
Provide each sentence followed immediately by its precise English translation in italics. Aim for clear, simple sentences; avoid rare grammatical exceptions.

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on if you can reliably produce short sentences using: у + genitive for possession, accusative after в/на for destination, instrumental with с for accompaniment, dative for recipients, and о/об + prepositional for topics. If you hesitate about case endings or mix up dative and accusative in recipient/destination roles, do another focused review of those specific patterns.
