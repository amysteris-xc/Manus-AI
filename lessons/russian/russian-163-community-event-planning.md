## Core pattern
We will focus on making polite requests and assigning roles for a community event.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Можешь прийти в субботу в пять вечера? | Can you come on Saturday at five in the evening? |
| Пожалуйста, помогите с украшениями. | Please help with the decorations. |
| Я организую столы и стулья. | I will organize the tables and chairs. |
| Кто может приготовить еду? | Who can prepare the food? |

## Concise explanation
Use the modal verb "мочь" (present: могу, можешь, может, можем, можете, могут) to ask about ability or availability: "Можешь прийти...?" is a direct, conversational polite question. For polite requests, use "пожалуйста" and the infinitive or the imperative: "Помогите с ...", "Поможешь ли ты...?" Note limitation: "можешь" is informal singular; use "можете" for formal or plural address. Avoid using English modal constructions directly — Russian often prefers infinitives or imperatives.

## Worked dialogue
А: Можешь помочь на мероприятии в субботу?
*Can you help at the event on Saturday?*

Б: Да, я могу помочь. Во сколько встречаемся?
*Yes, I can help. What time do we meet?*

А: В пять вечера у клуба. Пожалуйста, принеси столовые приборы.
*At five in the evening at the club. Please bring cutlery.*

Б: Хорошо. Я возьму посуду, а кто будет готовить?
*Okay. I'll take the dishes, and who will cook?*

## Guided practice
1. Translate into Russian: "Can you come on Sunday at three?" Use appropriate verb form for an informal friend.
2. Write a short Russian sentence asking someone politely to bring drinks: use "пожалуйста" and an infinitive or imperative.
3. Convert the informal "Можешь помочь?" into a formal/plural form.
4. Assign a role in one sentence: "I will be the organizer" in Russian.
5. Ask "Who can set up the chairs?" in Russian.

## Independent production exercise
Write a 6–10 sentence fictional event plan in Russian describing a small community picnic. Include:
- Date and time (one sentence).
- Two role assignments (organizer, cook, or volunteer).
- One polite request using "пожалуйста".
- One sentence confirming attendance.
- Keep language simple and accurate; use informal plural or singular depending on your scenario.

Example structure to follow (not in Russian):
1. When the picnic is.
2. Who organizes.
3. Who brings food.
4. Who brings tables.
5. A polite request.
6. Confirmation of attendance.

## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. "Can you come on Sunday at three?" — Model: "Можешь прийти в воскресенье в три часа?" (informal)
2. Polite request to bring drinks — Model: "Пожалуйста, принеси напитки." or formal/plural "Пожалуйста, принесите напитки."
3. Informal to formal: "Можешь помочь?" → "Можете помочь?"
4. "I will be the organizer" — Model: "Я буду организатором." or "Я организую мероприятие."
