## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to turn a simple direct sentence or yes/no question into a short reported sentence in Russian using the past-tense reporting verbs сказал/сказала and спросил/спросила plus a short clause (often introduced by что or an introductory question word). You will practice clear punctuation and keep the reporting verb in the past.

2. Recall Russian 096–101: Give the past-tense form of "to ask" for a female speaker.

## Core pattern
When reporting a simple statement, use сказал/сказала + что + clause. For yes/no questions, use спросил/спросила + (ли) + clause; for open questions use спросил/спросила + question word + clause.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Он сказал, что идёт в магазин. | He said that he is going to the store. |
| Она сказала, что урок начался. | She said that the lesson had started. |
| Он спросил, придёшь ли ты завтра. | He asked whether you will come tomorrow. |
| Она спросила, когда начнётся встреча. | She asked when the meeting will start. |

## Concise explanation
Form: use the past-tense reporting verb сказал/сказала/спросил/спросила (agreeing with the speaker's gender), then a short clause. Statements commonly use что before the clause. Yes/no questions commonly use ли after the reporting verb and before the clause; open questions use the question word (когда, где, что, кто, почему, etc.). Meaning: you are relaying what someone said or asked, not quoting them verbatim. Limitation/common confusion: reported speech in Russian often keeps verb tenses as in the original when the meaning stays the same; however, unlike English, Russian does not require extensive backshifting of tense in many everyday reports. Do not assume that all time expressions automatically shift—pay attention to context.

## Worked dialogue
А: «Ты придёшь завтра?»
*Are you coming tomorrow?*
Б: Он спросил, придёшь ли ты завтра.
*He asked whether you will come tomorrow.*

А: «Я купил билет.»
*I bought a ticket.*
Б: Она сказала, что купила билет.
*She said that she bought a ticket.*

А: «Когда начнётся фильм?»
*When will the movie start?*
Б: Он спросил, когда начнётся фильм.
*He asked when the movie will start.*

А: «Мы остаёмся дома.»
*We are staying home.*
Б: Она сказала, что они остаются дома.
*She said that they are staying home.*

## Guided practice
1. Rewrite the direct sentence «Я поздно приду.» as reported speech from a male speaker.
2. Turn the question «Ты видел мой телефон?» into reported speech using a female reporting verb.
3. Convert «Завтра будет дождь.» into reported speech: he said that...
4. Report the open question «Почему он не ответил?» using сказал/сказала or спросил/спросила as appropriate.
5. Take the yes/no question «Ты говоришь по-английски?» and report it with ли.

## Independent production exercise
Write 6–10 sentences describing a short fictional scene in which two friends report what a third friend said and asked. Use at least:
- two instances with сказал/сказала + что,
- one instance with спросил/спросила + ли,
- one instance with спросил/спросила + a question word (например, где или когда).
Keep all reporting verbs in the past tense and include English translations immediately after each Russian sentence.

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on when you can reliably:
- Choose the correct past-tense reporting verb (сказал/сказала/спросил/спросила) based on the speaker's gender;
- Use что for short reported statements and use ли for yes/no questions (or use a question word for open questions);
- Produce 3–5 short reported sentences with accurate translations and consistent past-tense reporting verbs.

## Spoiler: answers and model response

3. Он сказал, что завтра будет дождь. — He said that tomorrow there would be rain.
