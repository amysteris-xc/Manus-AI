## What you will be able to do
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:
- Tell someone your age using a natural Russian dative construction (e.g., "мне двадцать лет").
- Express simple impersonal needs using "мне нужно/нужны" to indicate what is needed and by whom.
- Distinguish the person who experiences the need (dative) from the thing that is needed (nominative or accusative, depending on the verb).

## Core pattern
Use two tight models:
- For age: "мне X лет" (dative pronoun + number + "лет").
- For impersonal need: "мне нужно/нужны + [thing or infinitive]".

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Мне двадцать лет. | I am twenty years old. |
| Мне семь лет. | I am seven years old. |
| Мне нужно купать ребёнка. | I need to bathe the child. |
| Мне нужен кофе. | I need (one) coffee. |
| Мне нужны документы. | I need documents. |

## Concise explanation
Form: The experiencer is marked by the dative case (мне for "to me"). For age, Russian uses the fixed phrase "мне ... лет", where the number is followed by "лет" (or the correct form of "год/года/лет" after numbers — see limitation). For impersonal needs, use "нужно" (singular/invariable) or "нужен/нужна/нужны" agreeing with the thing needed; the person who needs it stays in dative (мне, тебе, ему, нам, вам, им). Limitation/common confusion: In age expressions you must use the dative and the proper plural form of "год" (год, года, лет) depending on the number — this lesson keeps examples simple (e.g., "лет" is common for many numbers, but numbers ending in 1 (except 11) require "год" — e.g., "мне двадцать один год" vs. "мне двадцать один год" is often said as "мне двадцать один год", though colloquially "мне двадцать один" is common). Also, "нужно" + infinitive is common (мне нужно идти) while "нужен/нужна/нужны" is used with nouns (мне нужен карандаш).

## Worked dialogue
А: Привет! Сколько тебе лет?
*Hi! How old are you?*

Б: Мне тридцать лет. А тебе?
*I'm thirty years old. And you?*

А: Мне двадцать один год. Мне сейчас нужно идти на работу.
*I'm twenty-one years old. I need to go to work now.*

Б: Понял. Тебе нужен кофе перед работой?
*I see. Do you need a coffee before work?*

А: Да, мне нужен кофе. Спасибо!
*Yes, I need a coffee. Thank you!*

## Guided practice
1. Translate into Russian: "I am sixteen years old." Use the dative pattern.
2. Translate into Russian: "She needs water." Choose the correct form of нужен/нужна/нужны and the dative for "she".
3. Make a negative: "I do not need that" (use "мне не нужно" or "мне не нужен" depending on noun vs. infinitive).
4. Convert the sentence "Мне нужно писать письмо" into a question asking if someone needs to write a letter ("Тебе нужно писать письмо?").
5. Replace the person: change "Мне нужен карандаш" to "Ему нужен карандаш" and then to plural "Им нужны карандаши."

## Independent production exercise
Write 6–10 sentences about a fictional character named Оля (Olya). Include:
- Two sentences stating Оля's age using "ей" in the dative.
- Two sentences describing things Оля needs using "ей нужно" or "ей нужен/нужна/нужны".
- One sentence mentioning a task Оля needs to do with "ей нужно + infinitive".
- One sentence that negates a need (e.g., "ей не нужно ...").
Keep all sentences short and use Cyrillic. After each Russian sentence, provide an adjacent precise English translation.

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on if you can consistently:
- Produce "мне X лет" naturally for ages between 7 and 70 with correct dative pronoun and a reasonable number form.
- Form "мне нужно + infinitive" and "мне нужен/нужна/нужны + noun" and correctly mark the experiencer with dative (мне, тебе, ей, ему, нам, вам, им).
- Distinguish at least once in your own sentences between "мне нужно читать" (I need to read) and "мне нужен папка" (I need a folder). If you hesitate on noun agreement (нужен/нужна/нужны), review the Guided practice.

## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. "Мне шестнадцать лет." — precise model for "I am sixteen years old."
2. "Ей нужна вода." — precise model for "She needs water." ("ей" dative; "вода" takes "нужна" feminine).
3. Negative model: "Мне не нужно это." or with noun "Мне не нужен карандаш." — show both infinitive and noun forms.
4. Person replacements: "Ему нужен карандаш." / "Им нужны карандаши." — "ему" dative singular masculine; "им" dative plural; "карандаши" plural triggers "нужны".
