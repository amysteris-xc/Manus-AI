## Core pattern
Concise teaching paragraph:
Use лучше / хуже to express relative judgments (better/worse) often tied to personal preference. Use более / менее + adjective for more neutral comparative statements (more/less + adjective). For adjectives, you usually keep the adjective in its base comparative with более/менее; не all adjectives have irregular comparatives.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Это кафе лучше того. | This café is better than that one. |
| Этот маршрут менее сложный. | This route is less difficult. |
| Эта книга более полезная, чем та. | This book is more useful than that one. |

## Concise explanation
Form and meaning: лучше and хуже are short comparative words used like adjectives or adverbs to compare two items: A лучше B (A is better than B). Более and менее combine with an adjective to form a comparative phrase: более + прилагательное = "more [adjective]"; менее + прилагательное = "less [adjective]". Limitation / common confusion: some Russian adjectives have irregular single-word comparatives (e.g., хороший → лучше, плохой → хуже). You should not automatically replace irregular comparatives with более/менее if a short comparative exists and is natural. Also, выражение предпочтения (preference) is subjective — saying "лучше" often reflects opinion, not an absolute fact.

## Worked dialogue
А: Какой отель лучше — в центре или у вокзала?
*Which hotel is better — the one downtown or the one by the station?*

Б: В центре лучше для прогулок, но у вокзала менее шумно.
*Downtown is better for walks, but by the station it is less noisy.*

А: Я думаю, центр более удобен для туристов.
*I think the center is more convenient for tourists.*

Б: Возможно, но для короткой ночи у вокзала лучше остановиться.
*Maybe, but for a short night near the station it is better to stay there.*

## Guided practice — five numbered concrete tasks
1. Translate into Russian: "This museum is better than that one." Use лучше.
2. Translate into Russian: "This room is less expensive." Use менее + adjective.
3. Make a short Russian sentence comparing two buses: say one is worse because it's slower. Use хуже.
4. Rewrite the sentence "Она более внимательная, чем я" in English and then change it to express the same idea using хуже or лучше if natural.
5. Create a pair of sentences in Russian: first say "I prefer the earlier train," then justify with a neutral comparative using более or менее.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short paragraph (6–10 sentences) in Russian comparing two study methods (for example: reading textbooks vs. watching videos). Use at least two of the comparative patterns: лучше, хуже, более, менее. Include one sentence that explicitly states the speaker's preference (e.g., "Я предпочитаю...") and one sentence that notes the preference is subjective (e.g., "Но это зависит от человека.").

Note: Keep all Russian sentences grammatically correct; you may use simple vocabulary from prior lessons.
