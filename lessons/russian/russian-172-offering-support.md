## What you will be able to do
You will be able to offer simple, low-stakes help, ask for permission before helping, accept help politely, and decline help courteously in everyday situations (e.g., carrying bags, opening a door, small technical assistance). Focus is on short, natural phrases that respect consent.

## Core pattern
Use a consent-respecting question, then offer specific assistance, and accept or decline with gratitude.

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Могу помочь? | May I help? / Can I help? |
| Вы хотите, чтобы я помог(ла)? | Do you want me to help? (male speaker / female speaker) |
| Спасибо, я справлюсь. | Thank you, I'll manage. |
| Да, пожалуйста, помогите. | Yes, please help. |
| Нет, спасибо, я сам(а справлюсь). | No, thank you, I'll manage myself. (male / female speaker) |

## Concise explanation
Common forms start with a modal-like verb phrase "могу" (first-person singular of "мочь") followed by infinitive or a participial construction. "Могу помочь?" is short and informal but respectful if tone is polite; for more formal situations use "Могу ли я помочь?" or "Вы хотите, чтобы я помог(ла)?" Gender agreement matters in short past/future participles and some short responses: a speaker who identifies as female would say "помогла" or "я сама справлюсь." A common confusion: "могу помочь?" is fine for immediate offers, but it is not a substitute for offering detailed help in professional or emergency contexts — keep offers specific and low-stakes in this lesson.

## Worked dialogue
А: Могу помочь нести сумки?
*May I help carry the bags?*

Б: Спасибо, но я сама справлюсь.
*Thank you, but I'll manage myself.*

А: Вы хотите, чтобы я подержал дверь?
*Do you want me to hold the door?*

Б: Да, пожалуйста, подержите.
*Yes, please, hold it.*

А: Могу подключить принтер к вашему ноутбуку?
*Can I connect the printer to your laptop?*

Б: Спасибо, мне не нужна помощь сейчас.
*Thank you, I don't need help right now.*

## Guided practice
1. Translate to Russian: "Can I help you with the groceries?" Keep it short and polite.
2. Write a short Russian decline: "No, thank you, I'll manage, but thanks for offering."
3. Role-play: imagine a colleague looks confused at a document. Write two Russian lines: you offering help and them accepting.
4. Convert the neutral offer "Могу помочь?" into a more formal variant for a stranger.
5. Add gender agreement: write the sentence "I will manage" for a male speaker and for a female speaker in Russian.

## Independent production exercise
Write a 6–10 sentence fictional scene in Russian (use Cyrillic) between two people where one offers help three times in slightly different ways and the other responds once accepting and once declining. Include at least one formal offer ("Могу ли я...") and one informal ("Могу помочь?"). After each Russian sentence, provide a precise English translation on the next line. Ensure gender agreement is consistent for each speaker.

## Final self-check
You are ready to move on if you can: a) produce the offer "Могу помочь?" and a formal variant "Могу ли я помочь?" without prompts; b) decline politely with "Спасибо, я справлюсь" and its gendered alternatives; c) accept help with "Да, пожалуйста" and give brief specifics of what kind of help you need. If you hesitated on gender agreement or formal phrasing, spend another practice session focusing on those items.

## Spoiler: answers and model response
1. Model answer for guided practice 1: "Могу помочь с продуктами?" / "Могу помочь с покупками?" Either is acceptable; both mean "Can I help you with the groceries?"
2. Model answer for guided practice 2: "Нет, спасибо, я сам(а справлюсь." — include appropriate gender: "я сам справлюсь" (male) or "я сама справлюсь" (female).
3. Model role-play (practice 3): "Могу помочь с документом?" / "Да, пожалуйста, посмотрите, пожалуйста, эту страницу." — accept with a simple request: "Да, пожалуйста, помогите с этим абзацем."
4. Formal offer (practice 4): "Могу ли я вам помочь?" — use "вам" for polite/formal address. Criteria: use "ли" and polite pronoun "вам" rather than "ты" or nothing.
