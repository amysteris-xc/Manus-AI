# Russian 015 — Ability with `могу` and `можешь`

**Estimated study time:** 25–35 minutes
**Prerequisites:** Russian 001–007 and the immediately preceding lessons in this expanded sequence
**Author:** Manus AI

## What you will be able to do

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to say that you can or cannot do a familiar activity and ask a simple informal or polite ability question.

1. Say “I read at the library.” in Russian.
2. Ask “Are you at home?” informally.
3. Make **Я учусь дома.** negative.
4. From the previous C lesson, name one input or boundary check that prevents invalid data from being processed.

Check the spoiler only after making a genuine recall attempt.

## Core pattern

Use **могу** for “I can” and **можешь** for informal singular “you can.” Place a familiar infinitive after the form: **Я могу читать.** Negation goes before the form: **Я не могу читать.**

| Russian | Precise translation |
|---|---|
| Я могу читать. | I can read. |
| Я не могу читать. | I cannot read. |
| Ты можешь учиться? | Can you study? — informal |
| Вы можете работать? | Can you work? — polite singular or plural |

Read each Russian example aloud twice. Then cover the English and produce the meaning from the Russian. Keep the whole phrase together rather than translating one word at a time.

## Concise explanation

Start with the model sentence in the table. Identify the word that carries the new grammar pattern, then keep familiar words such as **я**, **не**, **читать**, **учиться**, **дома**, and **вечером** in their established forms. This reduces the chance of changing several grammatical features at once.

Russian word order can change for context and emphasis, but the neutral beginner order remains dependable: time or subject, then the verb or grammar phrase, then the remaining information. Use a question mark and Russian question intonation for a yes/no question. Do not invent an English-style auxiliary.

## Worked dialogue

> **А:** Ты можешь читать вечером?
> *Can you read in the evening?*
>
> **Б:** Да, я могу читать вечером.
> *Yes, I can read in the evening.*
>
> **А:** Вы можете учиться дома?
> *Can you study at home?*
>
> **Б:** Нет, я не могу учиться дома.
> *No, I cannot study at home.*

Cover the English translation. Read both roles aloud, then replace one ordinary noun or activity while retaining the new pattern exactly.

## Independent production exercise

Make two fictional ability exchanges: one affirmative and one negative. Use only general activities and locations, and do not reveal availability, health information, or real-world schedules.

After writing, cover the English meanings and read the Russian twice. On the second reading, underline the new pattern in every sentence. If a sentence has more than one unfamiliar change, shorten it and rebuild it from a familiar model.
