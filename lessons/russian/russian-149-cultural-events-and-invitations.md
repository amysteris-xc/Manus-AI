## Core pattern
This lesson centers on inviting and negotiating: "Would you like to...?", "Let's meet at...", and "Can we change...?"

| Russian | Precise English translation |
|---|---|
| Хотите пойти на выставку в субботу? | Would you like to go to an exhibition on Saturday? |
| Давайте встретимся в семь вечера у входа. | Let's meet at seven in the evening by the entrance. |
| Можно перенести на воскресенье? | Can we move it to Sunday? |
| Мне удобнее в девять утра. | It's more convenient for me at nine in the morning. |

## Concise explanation
Use "Хотите..." or "Вы хотите..." to make a polite invitation in present tense; this is direct but still polite in many casual-to-semi-formal contexts. Use "Давайте..." to propose a joint action ("Let's..."). To suggest changing plans, use "Можно (перенести/изменить)...?" or "Можно ли...?": these ask for permission or possibility. A common confusion: "Хотите" uses the plural/formal second-person form and is fine for one person as a polite form; don't overuse "хочешь" in formal contexts. Also note that time expressions in Russian commonly place the time before "вечера/утра" (e.g., "в семь вечера").

## Worked dialogue
А: Хотите пойти на концерт в парке в субботу?
*Would you like to go to a concert in the park on Saturday?*

Б: Звучит хорошо. Во сколько начинается концерт?
*Sounds good. What time does the concert start?*

А: Начинается в шесть вечера. Давайте встретимся в пять пятьдесят у главного входа.
*It starts at six in the evening. Let's meet at 5:50 at the main entrance.*

Б: Можно ли перенести на восемь? Мне удобно позже.
*Can we move it to eight? It's more convenient for me later.*

А: К сожалению, я могу только до семи. Может, встретимся в семь?
*Unfortunately, I can only stay until seven. Maybe let's meet at seven?*

Б: Хорошо, встретимся в семь у входа.
*Okay, let's meet at seven by the entrance.*

## Guided practice
1. Rewrite the invitation "Would you like to go to a reading on Friday?" in Russian and translate it.
2. Convert "Let's meet at 3 pm" into Russian and change it to 15:30 (use "пятнадцать тридцать" only if you already used 24-hour format before; otherwise use "половина четвертого" as a natural alternative).
3. Write a Russian question asking whether it's possible to change the meeting place to "near the café" and translate it.
4. Respond in Russian, declining and proposing a new time ("I can't then, but I can at 7 pm") and translate.
5. Compose a two-sentence Russian message confirming place and time, suitable to send as a short text, and translate.

## Independent production exercise
Write a short dialogue or message (6–10 sentences total) in Russian inviting a friend to a fictional cultural event (an exhibition, concert, or reading). Include:
- An invitation sentence with date or weekday.
- A sentence asking about their time preferences.
- A negotiation sentence proposing an alternate time or place.
- A confirmation sentence agreeing on final details.
Each Russian sentence must be accompanied by its precise English translation. Aim for natural, polite phrasing using patterns from the Core pattern.
